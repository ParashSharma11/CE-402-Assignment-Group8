{\small\bfseries\color{accent}CE-402 GEOTECHNICAL ENGINEERING --- GROUP 8}\par
{\LARGE\bfseries Engineering Audit of AI Output}\par
{\color{black!65}Calculation errors and omissions found in AI-generated code, how we found them, and what we changed}\par
\vspace{2pt}{\color{navy}\rule{\textwidth}{1.5pt}}

AI tools were used to write code: Gemini for the v1 platform (P1--P5 engines) and Claude Code for GeoFound (v2). The
engineering was ours --- we chose the methods and codes, set the parameters, made the design decisions and checked
every result. Each module was verified against hand calculations (\code{tests/hand\_calcs.md}), the two independent
implementations (v1 and v2) are run against each other (\code{tests/crosscheck.test.ts}), and the final design is
covered by 59 automated tests. This audit lists every calculation error and every omission we found in AI output, how
we found it, and what we changed. All items are traceable in the git history.

\section{Part A --- v1 platform (Gemini)}

\auditem{A1}{Direct-shear equation sign (P2, \code{js/moduleP2\_logic.js})}{When least squares gave a negative
intercept, the equation text put the sign between the two terms (``$\tau = |c|$ (raw) $-\ \sigma\tan\phi$''), so it
read as if the friction term were negative. Found by reading the calculation card against the fitted numbers. Fixed to
$\tau = -c + \sigma\tan\phi$ (sign on $c$).}

\auditem{A2}{Biaxial eccentricity $L'$ reporting (P3, \code{js/moduleP3\_ui.js})}{The factor table showed
$B' = $ \code{g.B\_eff} $= \min(B', L')$ but $L' = $ \code{g.L\_prime} $= L - 2e_L$. When $L - 2e_L < B - 2e_B$ the table
showed the same shorter side for both, a false square that contradicted $B'/L'$ in the shape factors (IS 6403 Table 2)
and $D_f/B'$ in the depth factors. Fixed to \code{g.L\_eff} $= \max(B', L')$ so $B'$, $L'$, $B'/L'$ and $A'$ agree with
the calculation trail under any eccentricity.}

\auditem{A3}{Net safe load synced from P3 (\code{js/state.js}, \code{syncP4FromP3})}{The settlement module received a
load inconsistent with the net safe bearing capacity $q_{ns}$, counting the overburden twice. Fixed so the synced net
load is consistent with $q_{ns}$ (net, per IS 8009 (Part 1):1976); added a water-table mode and a user-set IS 1904
allowable settlement.}

\auditem{A4}{IS 8009 rigidity factor missing on consolidation (P5, \code{js/moduleP4\_logic.js})}{$R_f = 0.80$ (rigid
footing) was applied to the immediate settlement $S_e$ only. $\Delta\sigma_z$ is evaluated on the centre line of a
flexible loaded area, and IS 8009 cl.~9.4 applies the rigidity correction to the centre-line settlement --- $S_e$ and
$S_c$ alike. Without it $S_c$ of a rigid footing was $1/0.8 = 1.25$ times too large. Applied $R_f$ to $S_c$ and
recorded it in the audit trail.}

\auditem{A5}{Schmertmann peak truncation (P5)}{$I_z(z')$ was sampled at sublayer midpoints, which cuts off the
triangular peak $I_{zp}$ whenever a sublayer straddles $z_\text{peak}$ (noticeable for 1--2 m sublayers). Replaced
with exact piecewise-linear integration split at $z_\text{peak}$, so $\int I_z\,dz'$ is exact for any subdivision.}

\auditem{A6}{Validation that allowed impossible soil (P5)}{The check $\gamma_\text{sat} \ge \gamma_\text{moist}$
allowed $\gamma_\text{sat} \le \gamma_w$ (negative buoyant weight) and gapped or overlapping manual layers, silently
corrupting $\sigma'_{v0}(z)$. Added $\gamma_\text{sat} > \gamma_w$, layer-continuity validation, and an advisory when
$C_r > C_c$.}

\auditem{A7}{Sub-day time rate (P5)}{The time--settlement curve started at 1 day and the $t_{50}/t_{90}$ markers were
limited to $\ge 1$ day, so thin or silty strata that consolidate within hours fell off the plot, and the time factor
$C_2(t)$ could be disabled by mistake. Curve start set to $\min(1\ \text{day},\ 0.5\,t_\text{target})$, marker bounds
follow the curve, and the time input stays active for Schmertmann's creep factor.}

\auditem{A8}{Stress-module load equilibrium (P4, \code{js/moduleP4\_stress\_ui.js})}{Syncing a footing from P3 used
the nominal dimensions, so $Q \ne q_0 A$ for circular and eccentric footings. Fixed with an equal-area square (side
$D\sqrt{\pi/4}$) for circles and Meyerhof effective dimensions $B'$, $L'$ for eccentric loads; custom inspection depths
are always kept in the table.}

\section{Part B --- GeoFound, v2 (Claude Code)}

\auditem{B1}{Footings wider than the column grid}{The sizing routine returned the smallest footing passing bearing and
settlement and reported it as ``sized'': $B = 6.00$ m for A6--D7 and $3.55 \times 5.32$ m for C4 --- on a 5 m grid.
Found on the plan drawn to scale. An isolated footing needs $B \le 5.0 - 0.3 = 4.7$ m. Added the geometric check; such
zones go to the alternative foundation (\code{tests/results.test.ts}).}

\auditem{B2}{Pressure-bulb labels at invented depths}{Isobars were computed correctly but labelled at hard-coded depths
($0.4q$ at $1.15B$, \ldots). Checked with the engine's Boussinesq centre stress for $B = 3$ m: at the ``$0.4q$'' label
the true value is $0.274q$, at ``$0.6q$'' it is $0.484q$. Labels are now placed on each computed isobar with its depth
($0.1q$ at about $2.1B$).}

\auditem{B3}{Shape factors after eccentricity}{The square row of IS 6403 Table 2 ($s_c = 1.3$, $s_q = 1.2$,
$s_\gamma = 0.8$) was used even when eccentricity made the effective area a rectangle ($e = 0.2$ m on a 2 m square:
$B'/L' = 0.8$). The rectangle expressions are now used whenever $B' \ne L'$ (\code{tests/engine.test.ts}).}

\auditem{B4}{Preconsolidation pressure}{The AI's own hand derivation (virgin line through the last two points, about
118 kPa) disagreed with its code (last three points, 114.6 kPa). Found by our independent hand calculation. The choice
is stated, the test asserts a band (110--119 kPa), $\sigma'_p$ is editable, and kg/cm$^2$ $\rightarrow$ kPa
($\times 98.0665$) is applied first.}

\auditem{B5}{SPT correction applied to clay}{$C_N = 0.77\log_{10}(2000/\sigma'_v)$ was applied to every record,
including the CI clay ($N = 6 \rightarrow 6.8$). Found by cross-checking against our v1 engine, which correctly leaves
cohesive $N$ uncorrected --- the IS 2131 corrections are for cohesionless soil. We also adopted v1's lower limit
$\sigma'_v \ge 25$ kPa in $C_N$.}

\auditem{B6}{Local--general shear transition read one way, silently (73\,\% apart)}{For BH-1, $B = 3.2$ m, v2 gave
$q_{nu} = 345.0$ kPa and v1 597.1 kPa. A term-by-term comparison found two hidden choices: strength of the founding
layer ($\phi = 30.0^\circ$) versus the average over the shear zone $D_f \rightarrow D_f + B$ ($\phi = 31.8^\circ$), and
interpolating $q_{nu}$ versus interpolating $N_c$, $N_q$, $N_\gamma$ for $29^\circ < \phi < 36^\circ$. Both are now
explicit options; with our chosen method v2 reproduces 597.1 kPa exactly, and the criterion ($\phi$) is stated in the
assumptions.}

\auditem{B7}{Design that stopped at ``fails''}{Every column was sized to its own minimum and the building checks then
failed (maximum 94 mm, $\delta$ = 68 mm, $\beta$ = 1/73) with no alternative proposed; the first pile layout gave one
pile per column, which is not buildable. We required a design that works: footings proportioned for equal settlement,
piles where footings cannot work, at least two piles per column, and a check of every adjacent pair
(\code{src/engine/finalDesign.ts}).}

\auditem{B8}{Concrete grade}{M25 was assumed ($E_c = 25$ GPa). Mandi has heavy monsoon rain and a water table that
rises every August --- alternate wetting and drying, which is \emph{severe} exposure (IS 456 Table 3), minimum M30
(Table 5), nominal cover 45 mm, footings $\ge 50$ mm. $f_{ck}$ is now an input (default 30) and
$E_c = 5000\sqrt{f_{ck}} = 27.4$ GPa.}

\auditem{B9}{No liquefaction check in seismic zone V}{BH-3 has loose saturated silty sand ($N = 6$, 24\,\% fines, water
table 0.6 m) 25 m from a stream, and nothing checked it. Added the IS 1893 (Part 1):2016 Annex F simplified procedure
(Youd et al.\ 2001): $\text{CSR} = 0.65\,(a_\text{max}/g)(\sigma_v/\sigma'_v)\,r_d$, CRR from $(N_1)_{60,cs}$,
$\text{FS} = \text{CRR}\cdot\text{MSF}/\text{CSR}$. BH-3 SM and SP liquefy at 0.18 g (FS 0.82 and 0.98); the BH-1 and
BH-2 sands pass at 0.18 g but fail at 0.36 g. Piles ignore shaft friction in liquefiable layers and carry it as
downdrag (132 kN per pile).}

\auditem{B10}{Water table at the time of the test}{SPT corrections and $\sigma'_{v0}$ for the clay used the August
design water table. The corrections normalise $N$ to the stress when the test was done, and $\sigma'_p$ comes from
samples taken in October. Now the SPT corrections and $(N_1)_{60}$ use the October table (BH-1 at 1.5 m:
$N''$ 17.0 $\rightarrow$ 18.9, no dilatancy above the water table), and $\sigma'_{v0}$ at UDS-1 changes from 79.5 to
92.0 kPa, OCR from 1.44 to 1.25 (the old value pushed load onto the flat $C_r$ branch and under-predicted settlement).
Bearing capacity, $W'$, Schmertmann and CSR keep the August table.}

\auditem{B11}{Cohesion intercept for sand}{The least-squares intercepts (SM 1.03 kPa, SP 0.37 kPa) went into IS 6403,
adding about 6\,\% to $q_{nu}$. For non-plastic sand the intercept is a fitting artefact; design $c' = 0$, intercepts
reported only.}

\auditem{B12}{Footings analysed one at a time --- the error that changed the design}{Each footing's clay settlement used
only its own $\Delta\sigma$. On a 5 m grid the pressure bulbs overlap at the depth of the CI clay: superposing the
Boussinesq stress from every footing within 15 m adds about 55 kPa in the clay (one footing alone: about 16 kPa), which
takes the lightly over-consolidated clay past $\sigma'_p$. Footings that settle 25--39 mm alone settle up to 114 mm
together (limit 50 mm). \textbf{The recommendation changed} from footings on lines 1--5 plus piles on lines 6--7 with a
movement joint to \textbf{bored piles under every column} (one foundation type, no joint, maximum settlement 13.1 mm).
A test keeps the single-footing result for comparison.}

\auditem{B13}{Moment in pile groups; C4 orientation}{Pile groups were sized for $P$ only. With the rigid-cap
distribution $P_i = P/n \pm M x_i/\sum x^2$, two piles under C4 would carry $783 + 261 = 1044$ kN $> 875$ kN; C4 needs
four piles (522 kN each). C4's footing also had the eccentricity across its short side; the long side is now along the
moment (area loss $2e\,B$ instead of $2e\,L$).}

\auditem{B14}{Pile critical depth}{$P_D$ was limited at $20D$ below the cut-off; measured from ground level (IS 2911
overburden) it is 95.5 instead of 109.5 kPa in BH-3, about 12\,\% less end bearing. Changed to ground level.}

\auditem{B15}{$c_v$ justification}{The AI called $c_v = 1.99$ m$^2$/yr (1--2 kg/cm$^2$ increment) conservative. With
$\sigma'_{v0} \approx 80$--92 kPa plus $\Delta\sigma$ the clay works at 98--196 kPa, exactly that increment --- the
value is accurate, not conservative. The wording was corrected.}

\auditem{B16}{Seismic zone hard-coded}{``Zone V'' was written into labels and the proposal text, and $Z$ could only be
typed as a number --- site data in code, against our own rule. Added a zone dropdown (II--V, IS 1893 Table 3) that sets
$Z$ and $a_\text{max} = Z/2$; all labels follow the input. Test: in zone II, BH-3 at 1.5 m is safe (FS 2.94).}

\auditem{B17}{Clause numbers without the code text}{The AI wrote IS clause numbers it could not check. Every such
reference is marked \textbf{TO VERIFY} in the code (amber chip in the app) and listed in \code{REFERENCES.md}; we
confirm them against IS 6403, IS 2131, IS 1904, IS 8009, IS 1893 and IS 2911 before submission.}

\section{Lessons}
The AI was reliable at turning a stated method into code and at bookkeeping (units, traces, tests). It was unreliable
wherever engineering judgement was needed: which water table applies, whether neighbouring footings interact, what the
site's exposure and seismicity demand, and when a ``fails'' result needs a design rather than a report. Each of those
was caught only because we checked the numbers by hand, compared two implementations, and read the output against the
problem statement.
